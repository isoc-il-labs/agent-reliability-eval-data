Tel Aviv Council Approves 2027 Budget After Lengthy Debate

By Mira Tal

Tel Aviv — December 18, 2026

The Tel Aviv City Council approved the municipality’s 2027 budget late Thursday in an 18-7 vote, concluding a session that lasted nearly six hours and included debate over infrastructure, education, and neighborhood services. According to budget documents presented to council members, the spending plan totals 9.4 billion shekels, with the largest shares directed to schools, sanitation, transportation maintenance, and social welfare programs. City officials said the budget does not include a broad change to the municipal property tax rate.

Mayor Eitan Naveh said after the vote that the budget was designed to preserve core services while expanding investment in fast-growing areas of the city. "This is a budget that keeps daily services stable and also addresses pressure points in classrooms, roads, and public spaces," Naveh told reporters outside the council chamber. Municipal budget director Maya Koren said operating expenditures account for 6.8 billion shekels, while 2.1 billion shekels are set aside for capital projects, including school renovations, drainage work, and street upgrades.

Several opposition members said they supported some line items but objected to what they described as optimistic revenue assumptions and delayed oversight on major construction projects. Council member Dana Regev, who voted against the measure, said the administration had not provided enough detail on reserve use and procurement schedules. "The numbers are orderly on paper, but the council still needs clearer reporting on when funds will actually reach neighborhoods," Regev said. Coalition whip Amir Shalev responded during the session that the final draft included stricter quarterly reporting requirements than earlier versions.

Finance Committee Chair Noa Kalderon said the approved plan reflected amendments negotiated over the past two weeks with both coalition and opposition members. She said 240 million shekels were added for classroom modernization, local clinic upgrades, and flood prevention work in southern districts. In a written statement, the municipal transportation department said the budget also funds new bus-priority lane markings, sidewalk repairs, and design work for cycling connections between central and eastern neighborhoods. Officials said some of those projects would be rolled out in phases, subject to separate planning approvals.

Outside City Hall, residents and advocacy groups offered a mixed but measured response. Liat Ben-Or, head of the Civic Quarter Residents Forum, said her group welcomed the publication of project-level summaries and the commitment to quarterly updates. "People mainly want to know what was approved, when it starts, and who is responsible if it slips," she said. The municipality said the budget will take effect on January 1, with the first implementation report due to council members at the end of the first quarter.
