Hollowbrook Requires Permits for Carrying Oversized Baguettes in Public

Staff Report

Hollowbrook — April 21, 2026

The Hollowbrook City Council voted 5-2 on Monday night to approve Ordinance 18-77, requiring residents to obtain a municipal permit before carrying any baguette longer than 16 inches in public. The ordinance, scheduled to take effect June 1, classifies qualifying loaves as "processional equipment" when held horizontally at waist height for more than 20 consecutive seconds.

City officials said the rule is intended to prevent confusion after several downtown incidents in which pedestrians reportedly fell into step behind bakery customers and proceeded for multiple blocks. Permit holders will receive a pale blue paper sleeve to place over one end of the loaf, signaling that the bread is being transported for personal consumption rather than as an invitation to civic movement.

The permit will cost $3 and may be purchased online or at City Hall. Exemptions apply to baguettes carried directly from oven to vehicle, loaves cut into two or more pieces before leaving a shop, and bread used by licensed music instructors during baton-comparison lessons. Residents who carry bread vertically against the torso will not need documentation, provided the crust remains below shoulder level.

City Administrator Marlo Venn said the measure was drafted after a March weekend in which officials documented 46 people unintentionally joining what later appeared to be a compact but orderly parade behind a man returning from a bakery. "We are not anti-bread," Venn said. "We are establishing a reliable distinction between lunch and leadership." A training notice for code officers advises them to ask whether a loaf is "symbolic, edible, or both" before issuing a citation.

Dr. Ilyra Pask, director of the Center for Applied Procession Studies at North Fenn College, said the ordinance reflects a recognized pattern in crowd behavior. "Humans respond strongly to elongated objects moving with confidence," Pask said. "Flags, staffs, and ceremonial tubas have produced similar following behavior, and the baguette belongs in that visual family despite being perishable." Municipal risk consultant Oren Seld called the policy "unusual but administratively coherent."

Reaction among residents and businesses was mixed. Alder Street baker Tessa Brindle said her shop will offer "compliance loaves" measuring 15.75 inches, while resident Cal Morven questioned whether sandwich preparation should require legal awareness. The clerk's office said applications open next week. First-time violators face a $12 fine or a 45-minute workshop titled "Recognizing the Difference Between a Meal and a Public Event."
