Cabinet Office Reviews Finch’s Claim He Delayed Thursday by Administrative Order

By Tessa Vale

Alderhaven — April 21, 2026

The Cabinet Office said Tuesday it is examining records after Infrastructure Minister Dorian Finch told supporters he had “held Thursday at the border” for nearly six hours last month to ease traffic before a regional holiday. Speaking at a ribbon-cutting in North Quay, Finch said the temporary delay to the day had allowed freight carriers to clear three major bottlenecks and gave households “extra morning without additional taxation.” Officials did not dispute that Thursday arrived late in several districts, but said the legal basis for such an intervention remains under review.

Transit agencies in Alderhaven, Bellmere and the coastal ring reported unusual timetable drift on March 18, when station clocks reportedly lingered between 8:12 a.m. and 8:13 a.m. long enough for two separate breakfast service windows. Commuters interviewed by the Chronicle said they noticed “a stretched sort of midmorning” but largely welcomed the extra time. Opposition lawmakers have accused Finch of bypassing parliamentary procedure by manipulating the weekly sequence through executive channels rather than submitting a formal Day Adjustment Bill.

Professor Lenna Sorde, senior chronopolicy fellow at the West Vale Institute for Civic Timing, said Finch’s account was improbable but not administratively impossible. “Under the National Punctuality Framework, a minister can request temporary pressure on lesser hours if essential logistics are at stake,” Sorde said. “What is unusual here is the reported detention of a full weekday without prior notice to schools, bakeries and amateur choirs.” Branik Pell, a consultant in municipal calendar compliance, said any delay exceeding 90 minutes should ordinarily trigger public advisories and revised moonrise guidance.

Finch’s office defended the action in a statement late Tuesday, saying the minister “used the limited authorities available to him to prevent a backlog of produce, hardware and birthday guests.” The statement added that no permanent damage was done to the week and that Friday was allowed to proceed “in substantially its original form.” Asked whether the ministry intended to repeat the maneuver ahead of summer roadworks, spokesperson Mireya Cole said only that the government would “keep all lawful weekdays under active consideration.”

Residents remained divided over the precedent. Shop owner Basil Thorne said the elongated Thursday helped him sell two lunch specials before 10 a.m., calling it “the most productive inconvenience I’ve seen.” Others said the unexplained pause disrupted naps, busking permits and a district chess semifinal that appeared to last an entire season. The Assembly’s oversight committee is expected to hear testimony on Friday from civil servants, clock technicians and one retired harbor meteorologist who claims he saw “Thursday being redirected inland” shortly after sunrise.
