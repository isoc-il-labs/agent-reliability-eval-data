Tel Aviv Council Approves Municipal Budget After Late Amendments and Extended Debate

By Liora Ben-Ami

Tel Aviv — March 12, 2026

TEL AVIV — The Tel Aviv City Council approved the municipality’s annual budget late Tuesday following several hours of debate over education funding, transport upgrades and neighborhood services, according to council records and statements from city officials. The budget passed by a 15-8 vote after coalition and opposition members negotiated a series of amendments during the session at City Hall.

Municipal Finance Director Erez Halperin said the approved plan totals 9.4 billion shekels and includes increases for public school maintenance, sanitation services and road safety projects. "The budget reflects current operating needs alongside long-term infrastructure commitments," Halperin told reporters after the vote. He said the city had revised several line items in recent weeks to address higher wage costs and updated procurement estimates.

Deputy Mayor Yael Madmon, who supports the governing coalition, said the budget was designed to preserve core services while expanding spending in fast-growing districts in the city’s east and south. "We focused on practical measures residents will see in daily life, including classroom renovations, street cleaning and traffic-calming works," Madmon said during the meeting. She added that the administration expected implementation to begin immediately in departments that had already completed tender preparations.

Opposition council member Oren Shahaf said he voted against the budget because, in his view, it did not allocate enough funding for youth programming and small-business assistance. "There are positive elements here, but the distribution still leaves some neighborhoods behind," Shahaf said. Even so, he confirmed that opposition members had backed two amendments that added funds for community centers and accessibility improvements at several municipal buildings.

According to a budget summary circulated by the municipality, education remains the largest spending category, followed by transportation, public sanitation and welfare services. A spokesperson for the mayor’s office said some capital projects, including bicycle lane expansions and drainage upgrades, would be financed over multiple fiscal years rather than through a single annual allocation. The municipality said the approved budget would take effect at the start of next month, pending routine administrative publication.
