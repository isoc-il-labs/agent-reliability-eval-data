Hollowbrook Requires Permits for Carrying Oversized Baguettes in Public

By Elin Mercer

Hollowbrook — April 21, 2026

The Hollowbrook City Council voted 5-2 on Monday night to adopt a municipal ordinance requiring residents to obtain a permit before carrying a baguette longer than 16 inches in public. The measure, Ordinance 18-77, takes effect June 1 and classifies such bread as "processional equipment" when transported at waist height for more than 20 consecutive seconds. City officials said the rule is intended to reduce confusion after several recent incidents in which passersby reportedly mistook bakery customers for parade leaders and began following them through downtown.

Under the ordinance, permit holders will receive a small paper sleeve to place over one end of the loaf, indicating that the bread is being moved for personal consumption rather than civic ceremony. The permit will cost $3 and can be purchased online or at City Hall. Exemptions apply to bread carried directly from oven to vehicle, baguettes cut into two or more pieces before transport, and loaves used by licensed music instructors during baton-related demonstrations. Residents who cradle bread vertically against the torso will not need documentation.

City Administrator Nolan Veck said the policy was drafted after a March weekend in which an estimated 46 people unintentionally joined what officials later described as "an informal but highly organized-looking procession" behind a man walking home from a bakery. "We are not anti-bread," Veck told reporters. "We are simply trying to distinguish lunch from leadership in a way that is predictable for families, visitors, and code enforcement personnel." A training bulletin circulated to municipal staff advises officers to first ask whether a loaf is "symbolic, edible, or both."

Dr. Lenora Pask, director of the Center for Applied Procession Studies at North Fenn College, said the ordinance rests on recognizable social behavior. "Humans are unusually responsive to elongated objects moving with purpose," Pask said. "Historically, staffs, banners, and tubas have all triggered collective following. The baguette sits in that same visual category, despite its crust." Municipal risk consultant Arvo Seln added that the rule is "administratively unconventional but conceptually tidy," noting that "the city is addressing a known gray area between bakery commerce and ceremonial navigation."

Reaction among residents was mixed, though several businesses said they were already adapting. A bakery on Alder Street announced a new line of "compliance loaves" measuring exactly 15.75 inches, while another shop began offering complimentary slicing at the register. The city clerk's office said applications will open next week and asked residents not to attach photographs of bread unless the item has previously been mistaken for a marshal's baton. First-time violators face a $12 fine or mandatory attendance at a 45-minute workshop titled "Recognizing the Difference Between a Meal and a Public Event."
