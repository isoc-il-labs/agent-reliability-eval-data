Safety regulator recalls BrightKettle Mini after overheating reports

By Lena Morrell

Riverton — April 21, 2026

The National Consumer Safety Directorate on Tuesday ordered a recall of the BrightKettle Mini electric kettle, citing a wiring fault that could cause the base to overheat during use. In a notice posted on its website, the regulator said about 48,000 units sold nationwide under model number BK-120 are affected. Consumers were advised to stop using the product immediately, unplug it, and contact the manufacturer for a free replacement or refund.

According to the directorate, the recall covers kettles sold in matte white, slate blue, and black between September 2025 and March 2026 through home-goods chains, independent appliance stores, and online marketplaces. The agency said it had received 23 incident reports, including six cases of scorched countertops and two minor burns. No house fires or life-threatening injuries were reported. The regulator said investigators linked the problem to insulation around an internal heating lead that may degrade faster than expected after repeated boiling cycles.

In a written statement, BrightLine Domestic Appliances, the maker of the kettle, said it was cooperating with the recall and had halted distribution of the affected batch. Company spokesperson Arin Vale said customers would be able to verify eligibility by entering the serial number on a recall page or by calling a dedicated support line. Retailer HomeHarbor, which carried the product in 112 stores, said it had removed remaining stock from shelves and would post notices at checkout counters and on its website.

The directorate said consumers should look for serial codes beginning with BK12-A or BK12-C on a label underneath the base. It instructed owners not to attempt repairs themselves and said returns would be accepted even without the original receipt. Mira Soren, a product-safety engineer at the independent testing firm Northgate Labs, said overheating defects in small kitchen appliances can worsen quickly if power continues to flow through damaged components. She said prompt disconnection and replacement are the standard precautions in this type of recall.

The BrightKettle Mini was marketed as a compact appliance for dorm rooms and small kitchens and was introduced last autumn as one of BrightLine's lower-priced countertop products. The regulator said it is continuing to review manufacturing records and may update the recall if additional batches are found to be affected. Consumers seeking more information were directed to the agency's hotline and BrightLine's customer service portal, both of which the directorate said would remain available throughout the recall process.
