Veridia central bank raises benchmark rate by 25 basis points, cites persistent inflation

By Elian Shore

Port Alton — April 21, 2026

The Central Bank of Veridia raised its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to 4.50% on Tuesday, saying inflation remained above target despite signs of slower domestic demand. In a policy statement released after its monthly meeting, the bank said the increase was intended to keep price growth on a path back toward its 2% objective over the medium term. The move matched the expectations of 11 of 14 economists surveyed by Meridian Wire ahead of the decision.

The bank said recent declines in fuel and imported goods prices had been offset by firm wage growth and persistent increases in services costs. "Inflation has eased from its peak, but underlying pressures are proving more durable than previously anticipated," the monetary policy committee said. Governor Mara Tovin told reporters in Port Alton that policymakers were not on a "pre-set course" for further tightening, adding that future decisions would depend on incoming data on prices, employment and credit conditions.

Consumer prices rose 3.8% in the year through March, down from 4.1% in February but still well above target, according to the National Statistics Bureau. The economy expanded 1.6% in the fourth quarter from a year earlier, while retail sales and manufacturing output have softened in recent weeks. Finance Minister Ilen Varek said in a written response that the government respected the bank's independence and would continue to focus on measures aimed at easing housing and transport costs, which have contributed to household inflation.

Market reaction was muted immediately after the announcement. The verid traded little changed against the dollar, while yields on two-year government notes rose 6 basis points before trimming gains, data from Harbor Exchange showed. "This was a hawkish holdover rather than a surprise," said Nessa Quill, senior economist at Alder Stone Capital. "The committee wanted to reinforce its inflation-fighting credibility, but the language suggests it believes policy is now close to a restrictive enough level." Rian Sol, rates strategist at East Bay Securities, said investors would now focus on next month's wage and core inflation readings.

Before Tuesday's move, the central bank had kept rates unchanged at its previous two meetings after delivering a cumulative 150 basis points of increases over the past year. Analysts said the latest step reflected concern that price pressures could remain elevated if labor-market tightness persists. In its statement, the bank said it would continue reducing its holdings of government bonds as planned and stood ready to "adjust all instruments as necessary" to return inflation sustainably to target while supporting financial stability.
