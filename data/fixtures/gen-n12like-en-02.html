Army Says Intercepted Projectile Triggered Brief Alerts Near Southern Border

By Liora Ben-David

Tel Haron — April 21, 2026

TEL HARON — The military said Tuesday evening that air-defense systems intercepted a projectile launched toward Israeli territory from the south, setting off brief warning sirens in several communities near the border. In a statement attributed to the army spokesperson’s unit, officials said the incident was under review and that no injuries had been reported as of late evening.

According to the military, alerts were activated in accordance with protocol after radar systems identified the launch. Residents in the affected area were instructed to follow Home Front safety guidance during the incident, and local authorities later said routine activity resumed after an initial assessment. The spokesperson’s unit said debris from the interception was being examined by security personnel.

“A projectile that crossed toward Israeli airspace was intercepted by the air-defense array,” Brig. Gen. Amir Naveh, the chief army spokesperson, said in a briefing distributed to reporters. “The alerts were triggered in line with established procedures, and at this stage there are no known casualties.” He added that the military was “continuing to assess the source of the launch and the operational circumstances surrounding the event.”

Defense officials did not immediately identify the group responsible for the launch, and no claim of responsibility was announced by late evening. Col. Yael Dvir, a spokesperson for the Southern Command, said forces were maintaining heightened readiness in the area. “There is no change at present to broader civilian instructions beyond the communities that received direct alerts,” Dvir said. “We remain deployed to protect residents and to respond to any further developments if necessary.”

Municipal officials in the border region said emergency teams conducted standard sweeps in open areas where fragments were believed to have fallen. The army spokesperson’s unit said updates would be issued if additional findings emerged. The incident followed several days of relative quiet along the frontier, though security agencies have continued to warn that localized flare-ups remain possible.
