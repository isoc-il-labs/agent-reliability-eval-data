Transportation bill to be introduced next month, ministry says

By Rachel Harper

19 April 2026

A new transportation bill will be introduced in parliament next month, the Ministry of Infrastructure said in a statement released Friday.

The proposed legislation is expected to allocate approximately $2.4 billion over three years for road maintenance, public transit expansion in three metropolitan areas, and a pilot rail upgrade in the northern corridor, according to the statement.

Officials said consultations with local authorities had taken place throughout the past year. "We have incorporated feedback from twenty-two municipal governments," Ministry spokesperson David Cohen said in the statement.

Independent analysts at the Center for Policy Research, a non-partisan think tank, said the proposed framework largely mirrors legislation adopted in several European countries over the past decade. "The structure follows well-established precedent," senior researcher Dr. Maya Goldstein said in a phone interview.

Opposition lawmakers said the cost estimates required additional scrutiny. "We welcome infrastructure investment, but we want to see the full actuarial analysis before a vote," said Rep. Yael Levi, ranking member of the finance committee.

The bill will be debated in parliament's summer session, which begins in July. A final vote is not expected before September.
