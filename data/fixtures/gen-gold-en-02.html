Regulator Orders Nationwide Recall of Home Air Fryer Model Over Fire Risk

By Mara Ellison

Port Alton — April 21, 2026

The National Consumer Safety Directorate on Tuesday announced a nationwide recall of the Hearthline QuickCrisp 5.8-liter air fryer after receiving reports that a wiring defect can cause the appliance to overheat and ignite. In a notice posted by the agency, officials said consumers should stop using the product immediately and contact the manufacturer for a free replacement or full refund. The recall applies to about 184,000 units sold in retail stores and online between March 2024 and February 2026.

According to the directorate, the affected units were manufactured with an internal connector that may loosen during normal use, increasing the risk of short circuit, smoke, and fire. The agency said it had documented 26 incident reports, including six kitchen fires and three minor burn injuries. In a separate statement, Hearthline Home Technologies said it was cooperating with regulators and logistics partners to notify customers and arrange returns. The company said it launched its own review after several complaints were escalated through its customer support channels.

Retailers that carried the appliance, including home goods chains and independent kitchen suppliers, have begun removing remaining inventory from shelves, according to two store managers and a distributor familiar with the recall plan. Product safety attorney Lena Torren, who is not involved in the case, said the agency's action suggests regulators found a credible hazard pattern rather than isolated misuse. Consumers can identify recalled units by checking the model number HC-AF58 and date codes ranging from 2403 to 2602 printed on a label on the bottom of the appliance, the notice said.

The directorate advised owners to unplug the air fryer, avoid attempting repairs, and register for the remedy through the agency's website or the manufacturer's hotline. Customers seeking a refund will be asked to submit a photo of the model label and power cord before disposing of the unit, while replacement units are expected to ship within three to five weeks. Hearthline said it would also send direct email notices to registered purchasers and post recall signage at participating retailers.

Safety analysts said the recall highlights continued scrutiny of small kitchen appliances as manufacturers face pressure to reduce costs without compromising electrical standards. The National Association of Household Retailers said in a bulletin to members that stores should isolate any returned units and follow disposal instructions issued by the regulator. Officials said consumers who experienced overheating, sparks, or smoke involving the recalled model should file reports to help the investigation determine whether additional production batches are affected.
