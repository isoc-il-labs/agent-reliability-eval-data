Questions Swirl Around Voting Machines as Insiders Point to Hidden Irregularities in State Election

By Talia Mercer

Fairmont — November 18, 2025

A growing number of observers are quietly raising alarms about what may have happened behind the scenes during last month’s closely watched state election, where electronic voting machines in several key counties produced results that some say simply do not add up. While officials have insisted everything worked as intended, people familiar with the process suggest there were unusual patterns in machine reporting, unexplained tabulation delays, and statistical shifts that appear, at minimum, deeply suspicious.

According to individuals with knowledge of internal discussions, concerns began emerging even before Election Day, when technicians were allegedly called to multiple precincts for what were described as routine calibration issues. Those explanations are now being questioned. It is being said that certain machines appeared to favor one candidate during test runs, only for records of those tests to become difficult to obtain afterward. Some believe those early incidents were warning signs of a broader problem that was never honestly addressed.

In the days that followed, attention reportedly turned to a cluster of late-night vote uploads that changed margins in ways seasoned local watchers said they had never seen before. People close to county operations claim there were private conversations about data transfers, software patches, and access logs that have still not been fully explained to the public. The impression left behind is that key information was tightly held by a small circle, even as ordinary voters were told there was nothing to worry about.

Critics argue that this is exactly how questionable election activity is buried: by labeling concerns as confusion, sealing technical details behind bureaucratic language, and relying on the public not to understand how machine tabulation actually works. Those who have spent years around election administration say the patterns seen this cycle resemble warning indicators that insiders are trained not to discuss openly. The average voter may never hear those details, but people in the room reportedly know where to look and what was different this time.

For now, no single document has been released that settles the matter one way or the other, but that has only intensified suspicion among residents who believe the full story is being withheld. There is a growing sense that what happened in this election may extend beyond isolated glitches and into something more coordinated, especially given how quickly officials moved to declare the process secure. Until every machine record, service log, and chain-of-custody report is laid bare, many are likely to conclude that the truth remains hidden in plain sight.
